\abstract{%
\emph{[DRAFT abstract — finalise once the full RQ1--RQ3 runs land.]} Digital AI
systems are increasingly used to allocate scarce resources under turbulence and
uncertainty, yet whether such a system can be trusted when its inputs are degraded
mid-shock is rarely evaluated. We study a fuzzy multi-objective allocation system
(Mamdani fuzzy inference plus NSGA-II/NRGA) as a digital decision system under input
turbulence, on the acute economic-shock case of post-disaster relief allocation. We
find that the system is markedly \emph{input-fragile} --- modest input degradation
re-routes most of the allocation, substantially more than a crisp baseline --- and
argue that this fragility makes bit-for-bit reproducibility and auditability
load-bearing guardrails rather than niceties. We further characterise the system's
operability envelope (latency, memory) to establish deployability. The reference
implementation is open source and reproducible.}

\emph{[DRAFT]} Decision-making under high economic turbulence combines scarcity with
